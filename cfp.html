Call for Papers

We invite research papers, position papers, benchmark and dataset papers, and systems and demonstration reports on agentic AI in the systems that move people. Submissions are due November 20, 2026 through OpenReview.

Scope

The workshop brings together the AI agents, autonomous driving, transportation, and human-AI interaction communities around a single question:

What does it take for agentic AI to operate reliably, cooperatively, and accountably when embodied in the systems that move people?

Submissions are expected to speak to one or more of the following sub-questions.

  1. Which agent designs hold up under the grounding, latency, and safety constraints of real vehicles?
  2. How should mobility agents communicate, negotiate, and coordinate at fleet and network scale?
  3. How should agents interpret human goals, sustain calibrated trust, and adapt to the people around them?
  4. How should agentic mobility be evaluated beyond driving metrics?

We welcome theoretical, algorithmic, empirical, benchmark, systems, and position contributions.

Topics of interest

Submissions are organized around four themes. Work that spans more than one theme is welcome.

Theme A

Agentic architectures for mobility

  • LLM, VLM, and VLA based driving and service agents
  • Memory, reflection, and tool use on the vehicle and in the cloud
  • Grounding language and plans in perception and control
  • World models
  • Levels of agenticness
Theme B

Multi-agent coordination and intelligence at system scale

  • Negotiation among vehicle, fleet, curbside, and traffic management agents
  • Multi-agent reinforcement learning and LLM-based reasoning at network scale
  • Agentic orchestration of ride-hailing, transit, and logistics
  • Mechanism design
  • Generative-agent simulation of travelers and cities
Theme C

Human-agent interaction and personalization

  • Goal interpretation and negotiation in natural language
  • Explanation and theory of mind
  • Personalization and long-term memory
  • Driver and traveler state understanding
  • Trust calibration
  • Accessibility

Key question: how should agents act when inferred goals and stated instructions conflict?

Theme D

Evaluation, safety, and alignment

  • Benchmarks that measure mobility agency beyond driving metrics
  • Hallucination, goal drift, and adversarial robustness
  • Verification, runtime assurance, and staged rollout
  • Value alignment, privacy, and accountability

Papers that surface open problems are explicitly welcome, for example which abstraction of agency is measurable independently of driving performance.

Submission types and format

Contribution types

  1. Research papers
  2. Position, benchmark, or dataset papers
  3. Systems and demonstration reports

Format

Submissions are limited to 4 pages excluding references and must use the AAAI-27 author kit format. Submissions may present unpublished work, work in progress, work concurrently submitted elsewhere, or recently published work.

Presentation and publication

The workshop is non-archival. Working notes of accepted papers will be posted on the workshop website with author consent. Most accepted papers will be presented as posters, and a smaller number will be selected as spotlight talks. The poster sessions are mentoring-oriented and are designed to give students and junior researchers a supportive setting to present their work.

Authors of the strongest contributions will be invited to submit extended versions to the Elsevier journal Artificial Intelligence for Transportation.

Work at an early stage, position papers, benchmark proposals, and negative results are explicitly welcome.

Review process

Reviewing is double-blind and handled through OpenReview. The submission link will be announced on this page. Each submission receives at least two reviews, and we target three.

Reviewers assess relevance to the workshop theme, technical soundness, originality, and the potential to prompt discussion at the workshop.

Submissions and reviews remain private. Only the working notes of accepted papers are posted, and only with author consent.

We expect at least one author of each accepted paper to register for and attend the workshop in person. The workshop is held in person only, and no virtual presentations will be offered, in line with AAAI-27 policy.

The workshop follows the AAAI-27 policy on the use of large language models.

Organizers and people with a personal conflict of interest with an organizer do not submit papers. Conflicts of interest are enforced in OpenReview.
