Human-Centric Agentic Mobility Services

Human-centered agency is the missing bridge from foundation-model agents to trustworthy mobility services.

February 22 or 23, 2027 (one day) Montréal, Canada Submission deadline: November 20, 2026
Call for Papers OpenReview (link coming soon)

About the workshop

Autonomy is the capacity to operate under internal rules without external control. Recent deployments of foundation-model agents in vehicles, fleets, and traveler-facing assistants show capabilities that autonomy alone does not entail: handling underspecified goals, engaging socially with passengers and other road users, using tools, and reasoning in ethically salient situations. These capabilities are captured by agency. Where autonomy concerns what to do and how to do it, agency concerns why to do it and what else can be done. The two are orthogonal dimensions of mobility intelligence, with distinct failure modes and evaluation requirements.

We use agentic mobility services for vehicles, fleets, infrastructure, and traveler-facing assistants that act as goal-directed, socially engaged, and accountable actors on behalf of travelers, operators, and cities. Agency is relational in a way autonomy is not. A mobility agent infers the goals of a particular traveler or operator, is trusted or distrusted by that person, and is held accountable to them when it errs. Human-centricity is therefore part of the technical specification of mobility agents.

This one-day workshop brings together the AI agents, autonomous driving, transportation, and human-AI interaction communities around a single question:

What does it take for agentic AI to operate reliably, cooperatively, and accountably when embodied in the systems that move people?

We welcome theoretical, algorithmic, empirical, benchmark, systems, and position contributions. The program pairs four invited talks with two poster sessions, two contributed spotlight blocks, a moderated panel, and an open roadmap discussion.

Topics

Contributions are organized around four themes. See the call for papers for the full list.

Theme A

Agentic architectures for mobility

LLM, VLM, and VLA based driving and service agents. Memory, reflection, and tool use on the vehicle and in the cloud. Grounding language and plans in perception and control. World models and levels of agenticness.

Theme B

Multi-agent coordination

Negotiation among vehicle, fleet, curbside, and traffic management agents. Multi-agent reinforcement learning and LLM-based reasoning at network scale. Orchestration of ride-hailing, transit, and logistics. Generative-agent simulation of travelers and cities.

Theme C

Human-agent interaction

Goal interpretation and negotiation in natural language. Explanation and theory of mind. Personalization and long-term memory. Driver and traveler state understanding, trust calibration, and accessibility.

Theme D

Evaluation, safety, and alignment

Benchmarks that measure mobility agency beyond driving metrics. Hallucination, goal drift, and adversarial robustness. Verification, runtime assurance, and staged rollout. Value alignment, privacy, and accountability.
