Human-centered agency is the missing bridge from foundation-model agents to trustworthy mobility services.

About the workshop

Autonomy is the capacity to operate under internal rules without external control, and the SAE driving automation levels deliberately describe vehicles as automated rather than autonomous. Recent deployments of foundation-model agents in vehicles, fleets, and traveler-facing assistants show capabilities that neither term entails: handling underspecified goals, engaging socially with passengers and other road users, using tools, and reasoning in ethically salient situations. These capabilities are captured by agency. Where automation and autonomy concern what to do and how to do it, agency concerns why to do it and what else can be done. Agency is orthogonal to that axis, with distinct failure modes and evaluation requirements.

We use agentic mobility services for vehicles, fleets, infrastructure, and traveler-facing assistants that act as goal-directed, socially engaged, and accountable actors on behalf of travelers, operators, and cities. Agency has a relational dimension that autonomy alone does not capture. A mobility agent must interpret the goals of a particular traveler or operator, earn and calibrate that person's trust, and remain accountable when errors occur. Human-centricity is therefore part of the technical specification of mobility agents.

Human-Centric Agentic Mobility Services (HAMS) is a one-day workshop that brings together the AI agents, autonomous driving, transportation, and human-AI interaction communities around a single question:

What does it take for agentic AI to operate reliably, cooperatively, and accountably when embodied in the systems that move people?

We welcome theoretical, algorithmic, empirical, benchmark, systems, and position contributions. The program pairs eight invited talks in four themed sessions, one per topic theme, with spotlights and posters on the same themes, a moderated panel that spans the four communities, and an open roadmap discussion.

Topics

Contributions are organized around four themes. See the call for papers for submission details.

Theme A

Agentic vehicles and in-cabin services

In-vehicle copilots and driving agents built on LLM, VLM, and VLA models. Multimodal context modeling and situational awareness across the cabin and the road scene. Grounding language and plans in vehicle perception and control. World models of traffic scenes. Memory, reflection, and tool use at latencies that vehicles require. Degrees of vehicle agency.

Theme B

Agentic fleets, transit, and traffic

Robotaxi dispatch and rebalancing. Agentic orchestration of ride-hailing, transit, curbside, charging, and logistics. Negotiation among vehicle, infrastructure, and traffic management agents. Multi-agent reinforcement learning and LLM-based reasoning at network scale. Generative-agent simulation of travelers and cities.

Theme C

Travelers, drivers, and road users

Interpreting and negotiating trip goals in natural language. Driver and traveler state understanding. Explanation and theory of mind in the cabin. Personalization and long-term memory of traveler preferences. Trust calibration in and around automated vehicles. Accessibility. Communication with pedestrians and other road users.

Theme D

Trustworthy deployment on real roads

Benchmarks that measure mobility agency beyond driving metrics. Road safety consequences of hallucination, goal drift, and adversarial manipulation. Verification, runtime assurance, and staged rollout of services at city scale. Alignment with traveler and societal values. Privacy and accountability in deployed mobility services.

Call for Papers

We invite research papers, position papers, benchmark and dataset papers, and systems and demonstration reports on agentic AI in the systems that move people.

Submissions

  • Three contribution types: (1) research papers, (2) position, benchmark, or dataset papers, and (3) systems and demonstration reports.
  • Up to 4 pages excluding references, in the AAAI-27 author kit format.
  • Submissions may be unpublished, in progress, concurrently submitted, or recently published work.
  • Double-blind review through OpenReview, with at least two reviews per submission.
  • All accepted contributions are non-archival. Working notes are posted on this site with author consent.
  • Most accepted papers are presented as posters, and a smaller number are selected as spotlight talks in the themed session that matches their topic.
  • Authors of the strongest contributions will be invited to submit extended versions to the journal Artificial Intelligence for Transportation.
  • Early-stage work, position papers, benchmark proposals, and negative results are explicitly welcome.
